About it

Illustration has always been where I give shape to characters I imagine could exist in a world where nature and technology live in harmony. Most of them belong to a solarpunk universe. It's a hopeful, hypothetical future where people, animals and machines share the same ecosystem, and technology is there to support life, not replace it.

The beings who live there wear specially automated clothing designed around their daily routines. It might help them tend their crops, care for the plants around them, or feed data to the robots that work alongside them. I'm interested in how these characters live as much as how they look. Some pieces also carry a quieter feeling. In one, a woodpecker sits alone at a table set for tea, with food and cups waiting for guests who never arrive. It's a melancholic image about loneliness, and a reminder that many birds are disappearing and need our protection. Even in a hopeful future, I want to remember what we could lose.

Each piece starts as a hand sketch. Many are inspired by the futuristic comics of the 1970s, with their bold colors, strange anatomy and optimism about what the future could be. From there I build the work digitally. I use Adobe Illustrator for flat, graphic compositions and Blender to sculpt figures and creatures in 3D, adding chrome surfaces, textures and light. In some pieces I use AI in post-production to add depth and atmosphere. It stays a finishing tool, never the starting point. The characters, ideas and compositions always come from my own drawings.

My background as an illustrator and tattoo artist shows are combined here, in form of ornamental frames, flowing line work and organic shapes. These are the same lines I've drawn on skin for years, now carried into digital space.

Idas das Vidas — Fanzine, 2025

In 2025 I launched Idas das Vidas, a fanzine that brings together my personal photographs and short poems about the cycles of life: growth, loss, return and renewal. It's a quieter, more intimate side of my work, but it explores the same questions as my illustrations about how we live alongside nature and time.

The fanzine premiered at the Fanzine Festival in Palermo, Italy, where I also exhibited my illustrations. Seeing my work printed and shown in person, and meeting other designers and independent publishers there, was one of the most meaningful experiences of my creative path so far.
